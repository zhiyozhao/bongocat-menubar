You type,
it taps along.

BongoCat Menubar is a little cat that lives in your Mac menu bar. Type with your left hand and it taps its left paw, with your right hand and it taps its right. No window, nothing in the Dock.

brew install --cask zhiyozhao/tap/bongocat-menubar

Not using Homebrew? Download the DMG and drag the app into Applications.

Free and open source. Runs on macOS 13 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Two ways to tap

Follow Mode (default)

Keys on the left half of the keyboard tap the left paw, keys on the right half tap the right paw. Space and the arrow keys count as right. Hold a key and the paw stays down until you let go.

Normal Mode

No left or right. Every key press alternates the paws. Half a second after you stop, the cat rests again.

  • Not typing
  • Left hand
  • Right hand
  • Both hands

Click the cat in the menu bar to switch modes. The app remembers your choice.

One permission on first launch

  1. Open the app

    The cat appears on the right side of the menu bar.

  2. Turn on Accessibility

    Click “Open Settings” in the prompt, then turn on BongoCat Menubar under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

  3. Start typing

    No restart needed. Within two seconds the cat taps along with you.

It hears keys, not words

Why Accessibility permission?

The cat needs to know when you press and release which key so it can move the right paw. macOS requires your approval under Accessibility before any app can notice key presses while you use other apps.

Is it safe?

The permission is only used to listen. BongoCat Menubar never blocks, changes or types keys for you, and it does not operate other apps. Turn the switch off on the same settings page at any time, and it receives no key presses at all.

  • It only looks at which key went down or up. It never reads the text you type.
  • Key information is used to pick the cat’s pose and then discarded. Nothing is logged or saved to a file.
  • The app has no networking features and uploads nothing.
  • The only thing saved on your Mac is the mode you chose.
  • This page has no analytics or third-party scripts. The demo above runs only in your browser.

FAQ

Opening the app from the DMG says the developer cannot be verified.

The app is not notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open it once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, click “Open Anyway” near the bottom and confirm. It will not ask again. Installing with Homebrew avoids this prompt.

It is installed, but I can’t find the cat in the menu bar.

When the menu bar is full, MacBooks with a notch hide the icons that don’t fit behind the notch. Quit a few menu bar apps you rarely use, or hold Command and drag unneeded system icons out of the menu bar, and the cat will show up.

I granted permission but the cat does not move.

In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, turn BongoCat Menubar off and on again. If that does not help, remove it with the “−” button below the list, reopen the app and grant permission again.

The cat does not move while I type a password.

That is macOS protecting you: while you type into a password field (or a terminal with “Secure Keyboard Entry” on), the system does not tell any third-party app about key presses. It recovers once you leave that field.

Do I need to grant permission again after upgrading?

Since 1.0.0 the permission survives upgrades. Upgrading from an older version needs one more grant.

I don’t use QWERTY. Are left and right still correct?

Yes. Left and right follow the physical position of the keys, whatever input source or keyboard layout you use.

Why do some keys do nothing?

The numeric keypad, Caps Lock, fn, Home, End, Page Up, Page Down, Forward Delete and F13 and above do not move the cat.

How do I uninstall?

First turn off “Launch at Login” in the cat’s menu (if you turned it on). Then run brew uninstall --cask bongocat-menubar, or drag the app from Applications to the Trash. To clear the permission entry too, remove it from the Accessibility list with the “−” button.

Does it cost anything?

No. BongoCat Menubar is free and open source under the MIT license. No ads, no account, no subscription.
